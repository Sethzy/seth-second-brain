# Conference playbook reflections and guerrilla marketing tactics

Source: Unknown

## Capture Text

Reflections / learnings to-date
Be clear on your primary goal; is it meeting current customers / partners / prospects or finding new customers / partners / leads or getting content / collateral
Most people focus on ‘creating leads’ but that is by far the most difficult goal
We’ve had the most success in using conferences to create partnerships / referrals; we focus on finding conferences where our ideal partner is exhibiting because it removes the difficulty of having to pre-schedule meetings / do cold outreach; if they have a booth you can just walk up
Don’t forget about that last one - content; if you have a content machine / audience already working, then the conference should generate a lot of material; “just attended xyz talk, biggest takeaways…” “here’s what i’m hearing at xyz” “just met with @john doe and had a great convo about xyz”; also photos of you and the team or speakers etc.
Most conferences have a hashtag that people use on LinkedIn, you can use it to find attendees if you don’t have the list ahead of time. Also easy to like their post and send a connection request
Relatedly, you should also post and use the hashtag, always drives a surprising amount of inbound from people doing the same
You should research all the exhibitors and sponsors and speakers ahead of time and make a target list - you can also send LI requests to all of them
In general, try to attend as few talks as possible - the value is in meeting people, not sitting and listening - but it is helpful if you have a big but cold lead that is speaking and can then call out a note from their talk in your outreach
You should use the conference to refine your messaging (to later flow into ad / web copy); have 3-4 different crisp one-liners and see what resonates most (you’re going to answer “what do you do” and “how is that different than XYZ competitor” a million times and it quickly becomes clear what is not working or confusing)
Smaller, focused conferences are better than bigger conferences in general, i.e., “mental health marketing conference” vs “HLTH”
The best ROI comes if you can give a talk / panel PLUS have a booth because then people will see your talk and have an easy way to find you later; just having a booth creates a bit of a cold start problem
One hack for getting to be a speaker if you have no connections is to message the organizers and say “I’m sure you’re fully booked up on speakers, but if anyone drops on a panel or talk, I’d be happy to sub in last minute; I’m best for anything at the intersection of HIPAA and marketing”
You don’t have to buy a ticket; you can just set up meetings at a coffee shop or bar nearby; I found this very surprising when I first heard it, but I’d say ~20-30% of people at a conference don’t buy tickets
If you’re scheduling a bunch of meetings, make sure to grab people’s cell phones / give them yours; there’s a ton of last minute rescheduling and it’s difficult to find people without this
Hosting events can be incredibly high ROI; we’ve had the most success with events that are: casual (happy hour not dinner; dinners are a big commitment for ppl and they often won’t commit; one of the best events I’ve seen was a happy hour at a bowling alley - was packed) + cohosted with complimentary firms (you each bring different people; pick firms that are bigger + more credible than you and sell into the same clients with a different product; half the value is then in just announcing you are hosting an event with them) + scheduled early and to be minimally competitive (e.g., doing it the night before the conference starts or just in general at a time when there aren’t 20 other events)
Many of my best connections at conferences came from when I was alone and had to knock out some work or grab a bite of food; instead of going back to my room, I’d always go find a roundtop or table or something where someone was alone and sit there; after sending a few emails / taking a few bites I’d then introduce myself and we’d chat casually; I never push it, but about half the time they’d ask more about what we do or it’d come up naturally and they often were a fantastic lead; same goes for when you’re walking to the conference from your hotel, easy to meet people in the elevator or at the coffee shop if you’re at all aware and open to it
Don’t be pushy / obviously working a room, that just sucks for everyone involved
If you’re not going to a conference, you can still get value:
Chime in on people’s posts that are there
Post something about going or not going with the hashtag so people find you in research

What I’d do differently…
Conferences have been incredibly effective for us; probably responsible for ~30% of our revenue in the last 6 months; that said, I think we could’ve gotten more from this
My strategy was low cost, slow roll, wide spread - going to 10-12 conferences, just as an attendee with the plan to then invest in a few in a big way next year
I’m not sure this was necessarily correct; there’s a very good argument to be made for going splashy; I think I underestimated how much partners and clients view your willingness to invest in booths / sponsorships as a sign of success and credibility
I think our credibility / speed to market would’ve been significantly improved by spending more money and time at one major conference; the challenge was that I did not know which one to do, but I think I could’ve figured this out via advisors
I also don’t think I spent enough time trying to get on panels and speaking
Ditto for direct prospect outreach


Guerilla marketing: 


"Guerilla Marketing Tactics Discussed:

1. Truck Advertising

- Rent and park truck outside conference
- Paint with company logo (~$3,000)
- Cheaper alternative to official sponsorship
- Can use digital sides or painted exterior

2. Strategic Location Control

- Buy out nearby coffee shop bagels
- Target unclaimed real estate around conference venues
- Focus on areas where attendee eyeballs naturally go

3. Example Case (Gaming Conference)

- Exploding Kittens placed stickers on urinals
- Generated buzz and conversations
- Drove hundreds of daily visitors to their small booth
- Successful despite limited budget"
