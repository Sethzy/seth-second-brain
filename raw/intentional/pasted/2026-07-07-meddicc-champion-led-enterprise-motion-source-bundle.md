# MEDDICC champion-led enterprise motion source bundle

Source: Conversation synthesis and public web references, 2026-07-07

## Capture Text

Seth asked whether Anthropic-style Claude Champions / champion-led adoption is a standard enterprise motion, and whether it should inform the DoorDash/OpenAI 30-day engagement plan.

Working synthesis:
- Champion-led motion is standard enterprise sales, but bottom-up GTM is not the whole enterprise motion.
- There are two related champion concepts:
  1. Sales champion: the internal person who helps the seller understand the organization, shape proof, and reach the economic buyer.
  2. Adoption champion: the power user/operator who helps spread usage, train others, and create internal pull.
- In enterprise sales, both only matter if they connect to an economic buyer, buying committee, business case, security/procurement path, and budget.
- MEDDICC treats the Champion as crucial for identifying and engaging the Economic Buyer; inability or unwillingness to introduce the Economic Buyer is a signal the champion may not be real.
- Gartner buyer-enablement framing supports the need for buyer-facing content that helps complex buying groups complete purchase tasks and reach value clarity.
- Gartner buyer-survey framing suggests modern B2B buyers increasingly prefer self-directed buying, but still value seller input for contextual judgment like fit to company needs.
- Microsoft and Atlassian both use champions/adoption programs to help enterprise rollouts spread and sustain adoption.
- McKinsey product-led sales describes the hybrid motion as combining bottom-up user/developer adoption with top-down sales to technology executives.
- For DoorDash/OpenAI, the clean phrasing is: use an operator-led, champion-enabled enterprise motion. Start close to the workflow, earn operator critique, turn that into a sharper POV and proof artifact, then work with the Account Director to bring a real champion and senior leader into a focused POC conversation.
