## The one idea behind every AI win

Every worthwhile AI improvement moves one of three levers:

- **Effectiveness** — makes the business more money
- **Efficiency** — gives the owner hours back
- **Quality** — makes their customers happier

Your entire job on the first call is to figure out which lever the owner cares about most right now, and the single task dragging it down. One lever. One bottleneck. That's it.

## Why it's two meetings, not one

This is the part most people get wrong. The 15-minute call is **pure fact-finding.** You ask, you listen, you name the problem back to them. You do **not** prescribe a tool live.

Why: prescribing on the spot makes you look like a guesser. Saying *"let me research the right fix for this and bring it back to you"* makes you look like a professional, and it books you a second conversation. Two touches beat one every time.

- **Meeting 1 (15 min):** Find the one bottleneck. Book the follow-up.
- **Between meetings:** Do the homework. Pick the one tool.
- **Meeting 2 (the prescription):** Deliver the fix. Open the door to paid help.

## Meeting 1 — the 5 questions

Work them in order. They funnel from broad to specific.

1. **The fork** — *"If AI could fix one thing in your business in the next 30 days, would you want it to make you more money, give you back hours, or make your customers happier?"* → tells you which lever matters
2. **Repetition** — *"What's the task you or your team repeat most often every week?"*
3. **Friction** — *"Where do things most often slow down, get stuck, or fall through the cracks?"*
4. **Quantify** — *"How many hours a week does that eat, and who's doing it?"* → this is your ROI anchor, write the number down
5. **Magic wand** — *"If that one thing ran itself, what would change for you?"* → confirms the problem is worth solving

**Then close the call:** name the single bottleneck back to them in one sentence, and book the follow-up. Example: *"It sounds like the real leak is the 6 hours a week you spend rewriting the same proposals. Give me a couple days to find the right fix, and let's talk Thursday."*

## Between meetings — pick THE one

You'll usually surface more than one problem. Pick the single bottleneck that scores highest on:

**Frequency × Friction, weighted toward the lever they chose in Question 1.**

Whatever is repeated most, hurts most, and lines up with what they said they cared about — that's the one. Resisting the urge to fix everything is what makes you look sharp.

## Meeting 2 — find the one tool

Use this decision tree to land on a single recommendation:

- **Is there likely an off-the-shelf tool for this exact task?** → Search [theresanaiforthat.com](http://theresanaiforthat.com) and [futurepedia.io](http://futurepedia.io) using their specific task plus their industry. Pick one.
- **Is it a judgment, writing, or research task with no clean tool?** → The answer is **Claude Cowork.**
- **Is it a repeatable workflow unique to their business?** → The answer is a **custom Claude skill.**

Bring three things to Meeting 2: the tool name, what it costs, and the first action they can take this week.

## The moment it becomes money

Deliver the recommendation for free. You promised a fix; give them one. That builds the trust everything else rests on.

Then open the door:

> "I can hand this off so you can run with it, or I can set it up with you so it's working by next week. Want me to put together what that would look like?"
> 

That single line is how a free 15-minute call becomes a paying client.

## Run this 5 times

The playbook works. The skill comes from reps. Run it on five business owners and you'll have your voice, your confidence, and likely your first client.
