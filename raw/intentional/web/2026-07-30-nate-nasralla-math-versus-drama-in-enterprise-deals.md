# Nate Nasralla — Math Versus Drama in Enterprise Deals

Source: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/natenasralla_if-youre-moving-upmarket-the-biggest-difference-share-7487264419660447744-CHFc/?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_desktop&rcm=ACoAAFklzP8BjEopwFb3X1DpQIoGPgEdps-D5rc

## Capture Text

If you're moving upmarket, the biggest difference you'll see is that, now, you've got a second type of problem to solve in every deal:
 
→ 1st problem: Math
→ 2nd problem: "Drama"
 
1. Math has a solution when you add, subtract, multiply / divide. So once you solve the problem, it goes away.

2. But Drama doesn't, because it's about people and the stories we listen to and create. 
   
And the thing is, perfectly-solvable Math problems go unsolved, because of Drama.
 
Here's an example:
 
An outdoor retailer's gearing up for holiday shopper support: sizing questions, return policies, gear specs, etc... and last year, they tried to get ahead of the rush by investing in 1,000's of content pages for customers to find answers. A massive project by the VP of Support.
 
Problem was, customers still couldn't find what they're looking for.

So they'd give up quickly, and ask to talk to a human.
 
1. First, the math problem: 

Customer inquiries > support staff = longer wait times + lost order revenue
 
The solution? An agent that understands the intent of a customer's question: customers get the right answer, *subtracting* from the # of inquiries, with remaining questions *divided* among existing staff, and *adding* back that lost revenue
 
Simple math problem, but, for the seller, it took forever to solve: 

2. Because of the Drama.
   
- VP Support who owned the content project didn't want to admit it "failed."
  
- Support Manager wanted to keep telling everyone he was crazzzy busy, and didn't have time to look at a new solution, to appear needed and valued. 

(The backlog = job security.)
 
- eCommerce Director was nervous he wouldn't hit his conversion targets, and wanted to shift blame to Support's slow responses / shopper questions.
  
*And now, here's the key*

Notice how the Drama was doing something for each buyer: the story helped them in some way, so, they were largely invested in keeping the same ol'. 
 
So when you have a right-fit solution and the Math checks out, BUT you're still blocked, chances are, it's the Drama you've got work on. 

Which often means helping each buyer believe a new story:
 
- VP Support: "My content project = context for any AI solution to work, I paved the way."
  
- Support Manager: "I'll be free to work on meaningful projects leadership *actually* values, not running the ticket treadmill every week."
  
- eCommerce Director: "My team has my back, they'll think creatively to make sure we hit our targets together."
  
We rarely see Drama for what it is when we're in it. Which is why it's our job as sellers to label what's felt, and connect it back to the Math.
 
(Yes, I totally learned this framework from my own therapist by the way.)

So: what's the Drama in some of your deals? 

Do you know it, and are you working on it, just the same as the Math?
