# Amanda Zhu — Manual outbound and authentic voice

## Verbatim Text

Manual outbound brought $4.1M this year and counting.

Last week a prospect asked if I was a real person.

My inbox is flooded with AI-generated outbound. It all sounds the same because the tactics are cheap to copy.

Reference my recent post. Sent at a weird time. Include an aside.

None of that matters. AI can do all of it.

What AI can’t do is sound like you.

What I’ve found to work and get responses:

1/ only compliment when it’s real. “Love your recent post” reads like a bot because a bot could send it. “The way you handled the pricing pushback in that thread was the clearest thing I’ve read this year” reads like a human with an opinion. If you can’t compliment something specifically, don’t compliment at all.

2/ the email has 1 job. be honest about it. ”I want to sell you our product. Here’s why I think it’s worth your time.” Not “would love to explore.” “worth 15 mins?” Directness reads human.

3/ write like you talk. If you’d say “I think you’re leaving money on the table” in a conversation, write that. “I noticed some potential optimization opportunities in your pricing approach” sounds like corporate-smoothing. Your actual voice breaks every template AI defaults to.

None of these are things you can prompt for because they all require you to actually mean what you’re writing.

-----

Here’s an email I sent earlier this year that led to a $120K deal:

Hey [name] - I saw you’re launching an AI notetaker and starting with Zoom. The question your team is going to hit in 3 months is whether it makes sense to continue building integrations for Microsoft Teams and Google Meet or use ours. I’d rather have that conversation now than after you’ve burned a quarter on it. Are you free next Tuesday at 1pm PT?
