# Matt Green — Status quo qualification questions

## Verbatim Text

51% of your losses went to a competitor with no reps, no product, no demo, and no quota. And these deals never look sick in the pipeline! They look GREAT. "Awesome conversation. Super positive energy. Moving it to commit." [Morgan Freeman narration voice]: It was not, in fact, moving to commit.

Status quo is the final boss of most deals, and its also one of the many, many....many reasons Jen Allen-Knuth was hired to be on stage 87 times last week. Reps lose to this more than anything else, and Jen is the best person to teach them how to beat it. Fortunately, she also leads our Complex Selling Certification here at Sales Assembly.

One simple thing she drills into our learners is leveraging 2 questions, with 5 mins left on the discovery call.

The setup, verbatim: "I hope Ive earned the privilege of you being completely truthful with me. Please dont tell me what you think I want to hear." (Yes, say it exactly like that. The formality IS the move. It tells them this one isnt a throwaway question.)

Question one: "Based on what weve covered, does this feel like a problem worth solving?" Then zip it. Count ceiling tiles if you have to. The silence is out there collecting an honest answer, and honest beats polite by about a million dollars a quarter.

Question two, asked separately: "Does this feel like a problem worth solving right now?"

- Yes + Yes: beautiful. The next step gets a date and a purpose before anybody hangs up.
- Yes + No: breathe. You never controlled their timing anyway, and now you KNOW the timing. "What has to happen first? Roughly when does this feel right?" A real date, never "next quarter." The deal EXITS YOUR FORECAST and enters nurture with a date attached.
- No + No: thank them, mean it, disappear like Homer into the bushes, and enjoy all the prospecting time you just got back.

30 slightly awkward seconds that save you three months of texting / emailing / sending carrier pigeons into the void.

With all that laid out, here's a project for ya - go pull last quarters slipped deals and check one thing: Did the buyer ever answer these two questions OUT LOUD?

Didnt think so. Start changing that now.

To be clear, youll hear "no" a lot with this. Bueno! A fast no is the question doing exactly what you hired it to do.

Here's a handy takeaway for you and your reps. Have them set it as their desktop wallpaper.
