# Nicol Pasuit — Deals die in the follow-up gap

## Verbatim Text

The most expensive deals in most tech company pipelines aren't the ones that were lost.

They're the ones sitting in the same stage they've been in for six weeks, because nobody followed up and nobody knew they needed to.

Most deals don't die with a "no." They die in the gap between the last call and the follow-up that never went out.

The prospect was interested. The call went well. Someone meant to send a next-step email that day, or the day after, or by end of week.

But then something else came up. The deal got buried under newer conversations.

Three weeks passed. Then six.

By the time anyone looked back, it felt like too much time had gone by, too much context had been lost.

This is what happens when a sales process relies on a person's memory to know which deal needs attention today.

Capability without structure produces inconsistent results. Some deals get followed up perfectly. Others fall through a gap that nobody designed, nobody intended, and nobody noticed until it was too late.

A structured sales process exists for exactly this reason.

When the process is defined, deals don't depend on timing or bandwidth or who happened to look at the CRM that morning.

The right things happen because they're built into the motion.

If your pipeline has deals sitting in this gap right now, let's talk about what it takes to close it.
