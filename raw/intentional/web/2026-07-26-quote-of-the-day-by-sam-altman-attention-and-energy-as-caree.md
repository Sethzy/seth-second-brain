Quote of the day by Sam Altman: “If you can't figure out what kind of work you like, pay attention to…”
2026-07-23T18:04:00+05:30
Many people spend years trying to answer a difficult question: What kind of work is actually right for me? The answer is rarely obvious, and it often changes as people gain experience, develop new skills or simply discover what keeps them interested. Career advice usually focuses on following passion or chasing opportunities, but those ideas can feel difficult to apply in everyday life. Sam Altman offers a different way of thinking about the problem.Instead of searching for a perfect job, he suggests paying closer attention to how different kinds of work make you feel. His quote encourages people to notice where their attention naturally settles and which tasks leave them feeling energised rather than mentally drained. It shifts the focus from external success to internal motivation, highlighting the importance of curiosity, engagement and sustained enthusiasm over time.Quote of the day by Sam Altman“If you can't figure out what kind of work you like, pay attention to what's easy to concentrate on and gives you energy vs. what makes you tune out and feel tired.” What the quote really meansMany people assume that choosing a career begins with identifying a single interest that will remain constant for decades. Real life is usually less predictable. Interests evolve, responsibilities change, and new opportunities appear over time.Altman suggests using attention and energy as practical signals. If certain tasks consistently make time pass quickly, encourage curiosity, or leave someone feeling mentally refreshed, those patterns may be worth taking seriously. By contrast, work that repeatedly causes distraction, boredom or exhaustion can reveal an equally important truth about personal fit.The quote does not imply that enjoyable work is always easy. Difficult projects can still be deeply satisfying. Instead, it distinguishes between effort that feels worthwhile and effort that feels permanently draining.Why Sam Altman's perspective attracts attentionSam Altman has spent much of his career working with entrepreneurs, engineers and researchers building technology companies. His experience includes seeing people succeed in very different roles despite having similar technical abilities. Those observations have shaped many of his views on motivation, productivity and finding meaningful work that matches personal strengths over time.That background helps explain why his advice tends to focus less on abstract ideas about success and more on observing behaviour. Rather than asking what someone wishes they enjoyed, he encourages looking at what consistently captures attention in everyday work.The idea is practical because it relies on repeated experience rather than assumptions about identity or future ambitions.About Sam AltmanSam Altman is an American entrepreneur, investor and technology executive. He first became widely known as the president of the startup accelerator Y Combinator, where he worked closely with hundreds of early-stage companies across different industries.He later became the chief executive of OpenAI, helping guide research and development in artificial intelligence while overseeing the organisation's rapid growth. Throughout his public interviews and essays, Altman has often spoken about career development, decision-making, long-term thinking and the qualities that help people build meaningful work.Although his professional background is rooted in technology, many of his observations relate to broader questions about learning, productivity and personal development rather than technical subjects alone.What people can learn from the quoteAnother takeaway is that awareness comes more through observation than through any eureka moment. Rather than looking constantly for the ideal profession, it may prove more useful to pay attention to those duties that tend to bring about curiosity and focus.Moreover, the saying suggests the separation of the results that come from outside and one’s own experience. An impressive job description or salary is no guarantee of an exciting profession; similarly, not very attractive occupations may turn out to be truly in line with one’s abilities, if only they always get one’s attention and keep one excited.Also, it makes clear that what counts more are patterns than individual experiences. Of course, everybody has bad days and dull jobs. But the overall perspective after several weeks or months will be more reliable than one formed during one particular assignment.A common misunderstandingWhile some individuals see this kind of advice as an invitation to shirk hard work and engage in only pleasurable activity, that is missing the entire point of the quote.This quote is not about how someone can evade any hard work. On the contrary, many fulfilling professions have their share of difficult tasks and failures. It all comes down to whether one can remain interested in the job regardless of those aspects.Altman’s comment is actually a call for noticing where the focus keeps returning, even if it is challenging to do so.Get the latest technology news and updates.
