# Abhishek GP — The boring documents that make AI work

## Verbatim Text

Most marketing teams I talk to that's struggling with AI adoption have the same unsaid problem: the highest-leverage work is unglamorous.

If you're on X, you'll feel a sense of FOMO that every rollout looks like a product demo. Agents doing magic, pipelines running themselves, a before/after screenshot.

Here's what it actually looked like on my marketing team:

1. plain-text files describing our ICP, messaging per persona per job title per vertical, win themes, and what's true this quarter. Lots of work.
2. pain quotes pulled out of our won-deal sales calls and distilled into the 20+ jobs buyers actually hired us for. Time spent reading transcripts, not just summaries.
3. a file of words, phrases and AI-slop we ban from our own copy. Always evolving, always catching.
4. a running log of every edit I make to an AI draft. It gets mined monthly for what to change in the system.

None of this looks like AI. But all of it is why the AI works.

The model is a genius with amnesia: it wakes up brilliant and blank every session, and those boring documents are its entire knowledge of our business.

I think this is exactly why adoption is stalling in most marketing teams. You can't demo a folder of documents. The team shows up expecting magic and you hand them homework. So leaders skip the conversation, buy another tool, and wonder why the output reads generic.

The line I like to use: this is the fastest, smartest hire you will ever make, and it knows nothing about us. Treat every boring document you write as its onboarding.

You skip the onboarding and you get intern output at genius speed.

Here's one self-test for you and your team: if your AI rollout hasn't produced a single boring document yet, you aren't producing highest-quality output yet.

Best deep dive I've found on the mechanics of this is Jacob Dietle on Noah Adelstein's The GTM Engineer podcast. He builds these context systems for GTM teams for a living, and the episode covers what the boring documents actually are. Link in the comments.
