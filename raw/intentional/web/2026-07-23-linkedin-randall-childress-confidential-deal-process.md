# Randall Childress — Confidentiality in a business sale

## Verbatim Text

The fastest way to kill a deal worth tens of millions of dollars is remarkably simple:

Tell someone you’re selling.

A business owner gets excited. Mentions a potential sale at dinner. Tells a vendor “in confidence.” Lets it slip to an employee.

Within weeks, staff are updating résumés. Vendors are hedging. Customers start exploring alternatives.

And the buyer sees a business that looks like it’s unraveling even if it’s thriving.

Here’s what most people miss:

Selling a business is nothing like selling a house.

A “For Sale” sign on a home means you’re moving up.

Word that a business is for sale means you’re going under.

Fair or not, that’s the perception.

This stage is where discipline matters most.

You’re not posting listings.

You’re quietly identifying motivated buyers, drafting NDAs, and presenting immaculate financials to a vetted shortlist.

Nine times out of 10, the buyer is your competitor. And that’s a good thing; they already understand what you’ve built.

Zip it until the deal is done.

Invest in the presentation like it’s the final pitch of your career.

Because it is.
