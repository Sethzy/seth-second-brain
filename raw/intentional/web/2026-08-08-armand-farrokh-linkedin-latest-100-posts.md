Don't bring power into a deal too early.

Let's pretend you have a first discovery call with a champion.

You sense that they agree on the problem, but need to see a bit more to truly be your advocate.

But your manager tells you get to power early.

You get the CXO on the next meeting.

- 15 minutes of discovery (goes fine). 
- 15 minutes of harbor tour demo (goes fine)
- ...then you trip a technical objection with the champion.

Now your champion is now trying to save face because they realize THEY'RE not even sure if they should've put this in front of their executive yet.

That is because they are not truly a champion yet.

You want your champion to be selling ON THE SAME SIDE going into power.

That means you either:

1. Get them to a yes before getting to power.

2. Or run it as an exec discovery call, then get exec sponsorship to run an evaluation process with their lieutenants.

Win rates peak when you bring an executive in on the 3rd call.

That is how you prove you know how to get a deal done.

1️⃣ 𝟑𝟎 𝐒𝐞𝐜𝐨𝐧𝐝 𝐌𝐚𝐫𝐤: 𝐆𝐞𝐭 𝐏𝐞𝐫𝐦𝐢𝐬𝐬𝐢𝐨𝐧 𝐓𝐨 𝐏𝐢𝐭𝐜𝐡

Most cold calls end when reps ask "how's your day going" or "did I catch you at a bad time" as they try to use pleasantries to mask the cold call.

Instead, call it out! Get permission to pitch to avoid the quick hangup:

""𝘌𝘮𝘮𝘢, 𝘐 𝘫𝘶𝘴𝘵 𝘧𝘪𝘯𝘪𝘴𝘩𝘦𝘥 𝘳𝘦𝘢𝘥𝘪𝘯𝘨 𝘺𝘰𝘶𝘳 𝘱𝘳𝘦𝘴𝘴 𝘳𝘦𝘭𝘦𝘢𝘴𝘦 𝘢𝘣𝘰𝘶𝘵 𝘺𝘰𝘶𝘳 𝘯𝘦𝘸 𝘰𝘧𝘧𝘪𝘤𝘦 𝘰𝘱𝘦𝘯𝘪𝘯𝘨. 𝘐’𝘮 𝘨𝘰𝘯𝘯𝘢 𝘣𝘦 𝘩𝘰𝘯𝘦𝘴𝘵, 𝘵𝘩𝘪𝘴 𝘪𝘴 𝘢 𝘤𝘰𝘭𝘥 𝘤𝘢𝘭𝘭, 𝘣𝘶𝘵 𝘪𝘵 𝘪𝘴 𝘢 𝘸𝘦𝘭𝘭-𝘳𝘦𝘴𝘦𝘢𝘳𝘤𝘩𝘦𝘥 𝘰𝘯𝘦. 

𝘊𝘢𝘯 𝘐 𝘨𝘦𝘵 30 𝘴𝘦𝘤𝘰𝘯𝘥𝘴 𝘵𝘰 𝘵𝘦𝘭𝘭 𝘺𝘰𝘶 𝘸𝘩𝘺 𝘵𝘩𝘢𝘵 𝘱𝘳𝘦𝘴𝘴 𝘳𝘦𝘭𝘦𝘢𝘴𝘦 𝘱𝘳𝘰𝘮𝘱𝘵𝘦𝘥 𝘮𝘦 𝘵𝘰 𝘤𝘢𝘭𝘭 𝘺𝘰𝘶 𝘴𝘱𝘦𝘤𝘪𝘧𝘪𝘤𝘢𝘭𝘭𝘺, 𝘵𝘩𝘦𝘯 𝘺𝘰𝘶 𝘤𝘢𝘯 𝘵𝘦𝘭𝘭 𝘮𝘦 𝘸𝘩𝘦𝘵𝘩𝘦𝘳 𝘰𝘳 𝘯𝘰𝘵 𝘪𝘵 𝘮𝘢𝘬𝘦𝘴 𝘴𝘦𝘯𝘴𝘦 𝘧𝘰𝘳 𝘶𝘴 𝘵𝘰 𝘴𝘱𝘦𝘢𝘬?""

2️⃣ 𝟔𝟎 𝐒𝐞𝐜𝐨𝐧𝐝 𝐌𝐚𝐫𝐤: 𝐀𝐬𝐤 𝐅𝐨𝐫 𝐈𝐧𝐭𝐞𝐫𝐞𝐬𝐭, 𝐍𝐨𝐭 𝐓𝐡𝐞 𝐌𝐞𝐞𝐭𝐢𝐧𝐠

Meetings sound really heavy. Prospects will reject you even if they're moderately interested because you're asking for too much at once.

Validate interest. THEN schedule the meeting:

""𝘔𝘺 𝘨𝘶𝘦𝘴𝘴 𝘪𝘴 𝘺𝘰𝘶'𝘳𝘦 𝘢𝘭𝘭 𝘴𝘦𝘵. 𝘉𝘶𝘵 𝘰𝘱𝘦𝘯 𝘵𝘰 𝘵𝘢𝘬𝘪𝘯𝘨 𝘢 𝘭𝘰𝘰𝘬, 𝘯𝘰𝘵 𝘧𝘰𝘳 𝘯𝘰𝘸, 𝘣𝘶𝘵 𝘫𝘶𝘴𝘵 𝘴𝘰 𝘺𝘰𝘶 𝘬𝘯𝘰𝘸 𝘸𝘩𝘢𝘵'𝘴 𝘰𝘶𝘵 𝘵𝘩𝘦𝘳𝘦?""

3️⃣ 𝟗𝟎 𝐒𝐞𝐜𝐨𝐧𝐝 𝐌𝐚𝐫𝐤: 𝐀𝐬𝐤 𝐀 𝐐𝐮𝐞𝐬𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧 𝐁𝐞𝐟𝐨𝐫𝐞 𝐇𝐚𝐧𝐝𝐥𝐢𝐧𝐠 𝐓𝐡𝐞 𝐎𝐛𝐣𝐞𝐜𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧

Most objections are reactions, not objections -- and when you fight a reaction, they only push back MORE.

Instead, agree with the objection and incentivize conversation with a question to get them talking:

""𝘚𝘩𝘰𝘰𝘵, 𝘮𝘺 𝘣𝘢𝘥 𝘕𝘪𝘤𝘬. 𝘐 𝘨𝘶𝘦𝘴𝘴 𝘦𝘪𝘵𝘩𝘦𝘳 𝘮𝘺 𝘱𝘪𝘵𝘤𝘩 𝘸𝘢𝘴 𝘳𝘦𝘢𝘭𝘭𝘺 𝘤𝘳𝘶𝘮𝘮𝘺 𝘰𝘳 𝘺𝘰𝘶 𝘸𝘰𝘶𝘭𝘥’𝘷𝘦 𝘳𝘦𝘢𝘤𝘩𝘦𝘥 𝘰𝘶𝘵 𝘵𝘰 𝘶𝘴 𝘪𝘧 𝘺𝘰𝘶 𝘸𝘦𝘳𝘦 𝘪𝘯𝘵𝘦𝘳𝘦𝘴𝘵𝘦𝘥.

𝘑𝘶𝘴𝘵 𝘴𝘰 𝘯𝘰 𝘰𝘯𝘦 𝘤𝘢𝘭𝘭𝘴 𝘺𝘰𝘶 𝘢𝘨𝘢𝘪𝘯, 𝘤𝘰𝘶𝘭𝘥 𝘺𝘰𝘶 𝘣𝘦 𝘣𝘳𝘶𝘵𝘢𝘭𝘭𝘺 𝘩𝘰𝘯𝘦𝘴𝘵 𝘸𝘪𝘵𝘩 𝘮𝘦… 𝘐𝘴 𝘪𝘵 𝘵𝘩𝘢𝘵 𝘺𝘰𝘶’𝘷𝘦 𝘨𝘰𝘵 𝘢 𝘴𝘰𝘭𝘶𝘵𝘪𝘰𝘯, 𝘺𝘰𝘶’𝘳𝘦 𝘯𝘰𝘵 𝘵𝘩𝘪𝘯𝘬𝘪𝘯𝘨 𝘢𝘣𝘰𝘶𝘵 𝘵𝘩𝘪𝘴 𝘱𝘳𝘰𝘣𝘭𝘦𝘮 𝘢𝘵 𝘢𝘭𝘭, 𝘰𝘳 𝘺𝘰𝘶 𝘫𝘶𝘴𝘵 𝘩𝘢𝘵𝘦 𝘨𝘦𝘵𝘵𝘪𝘯𝘨 𝘤𝘰𝘭𝘥 𝘤𝘢𝘭𝘭𝘴?""

***

And then for every objection moving forward... keep agreeing and incentivizing conversation BEFORE you handle the objection.

If you can get past 3 objections, they'll almost always agree to the meeting.

As a VP of Sales, I've heard hundreds of cringey sales calls -- here are the 4 keys to making discovery NOT sound like an interrogation:

1️⃣ 𝐔𝐬𝐞 𝐕𝐞𝐫𝐭𝐢𝐜𝐚𝐥 𝐐𝐮𝐞𝐬𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧𝐬 𝐨𝐯𝐞𝐫 𝐇𝐨𝐫𝐢𝐳𝐨𝐧𝐭𝐚𝐥 𝐐𝐮𝐞𝐬𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧𝐬

- How do you do X
- How do you do Y
- How do you do Z

- How do you run comp reviews?
- Typically when comp's on spreadsheets that means X or Y, which is it for you?
- Sounds like Y is a problem. When'd you realize that was a problem?

These not only demonstrate that your listening, but get you into 1 DEEP problem instead of 3 SHALLOW problems.

2️⃣ 𝐔𝐬𝐞 𝐏𝐥𝐚𝐲𝐛𝐚𝐜𝐤𝐬 𝐖𝐡𝐞𝐧 𝐏𝐫𝐨𝐬𝐩𝐞𝐜𝐭𝐬 𝐆𝐢𝐯𝐞 𝐘𝐨𝐮 𝟐+ 𝐏𝐫𝐨𝐛𝐥𝐞𝐦𝐬

Playback the 3 problems so that they know you're actually listening.

Then isolate the 1 that matters the most first.

3️⃣ 𝐔𝐬𝐞 "𝐏𝐢𝐥𝐞-𝐨𝐧𝐬" 𝐭𝐨 𝐬𝐡𝐨𝐰 𝐲𝐨𝐮 𝐮𝐧𝐝𝐞𝐫𝐬𝐭𝐚𝐧𝐝 𝐭𝐡𝐞 𝐩𝐫𝐨𝐛𝐥𝐞𝐦

--> Prospect (Problem): Ugh, these employees don't realize that we pay so much money for benefits and it's not just about salary!

--> Rep (Pile-on): I know. I remember the first time I got a cobra letter... it was freaking $800 a month for my own health care.

--> Prospect (Elated): I KNOW!!!!!
