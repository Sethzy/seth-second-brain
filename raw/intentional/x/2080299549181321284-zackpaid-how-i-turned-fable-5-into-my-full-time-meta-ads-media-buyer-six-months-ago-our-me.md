## Verbatim Text

how i turned fable 5 into my full-time meta ads media buyer

six months ago, our media buying function required two full-time employees across the accounts we manage. daily dashboard reviews, creative performance analysis, fatigue detection, budget reallocation, iteration decisions, reporting.

today, fable 5 handles 85% of that workload. the two media buyers still exist on the team, but their role has shifted from execution to quality control. they review fable 5's output, approve or override its recommendations, and handle the edge cases the agent flags for human judgment.

the shift didn't happen because fable 5 is smarter than a human media buyer. it happened because the media buying function is 85% pattern recognition and decision logic and 15% strategic judgment. fable 5 runs the 85% faster, more consistently, and at a lower error rate than any human doing the same tasks for 8 hours a day across 15+ accounts.

here's how the system works.

## what the agent is briefed on (the context layer)

fable 5 doesn't operate in a vacuum. the output quality is a direct function of the input quality. feed it a vague brief and the output is generic. feed it the right context and the output matches what a senior media buyer would produce after a week of studying the account.

the briefing document has five components:

1. ICP definition: the full extraction from the sonnet 5 offer engineering process. pain points, desired outcomes, buying triggers, failed past solutions, emotional texture. fable 5 needs this to evaluate whether a creative angle is speaking to the right person at the right awareness stage.

2. offer structure: the current offer, the guarantee, the pricing, the mechanism description. fable 5 needs this to assess whether a creative's messaging is aligned with the offer or drifting into generic territory.

3. account performance baseline: last 30/60/90 days of performance data by campaign, ad set, and creative. CTR, CPL, ROAS, thumbstop rate, frequency, CPM. broken down by hook category, format type, and awareness stage using the naming convention.

4. winning angle data: which buying triggers have historically performed, which have failed, which haven't been tested yet. the pattern data from the feedback loop.

5. current creative batch: what's running right now, when each creative launched, and the daily performance trend for each one over the last 7 days.

this briefing document gets updated weekly. the update takes 20 minutes because most of the data is pulled automatically through the meta MCP connection.

## what fable 5 produces (the output layer)

with the briefing loaded, fable 5 runs five core functions that previously required human execution:

function 1: daily performance monitoring

fable 5 reviews every active creative across every account on a defined cadence (we run it every 4 hours during peak spend windows, every 8 hours during off-peak). for each creative, it evaluates the four fatigue signals:

- CPM trending up 20%+ week-over-week while impressions hold

- CTR declining 15%+ from peak over 5+ consecutive days

- thumbstop below 25% after previously clearing 35%

- frequency above 3.0 on a prospecting campaign

when two or more signals fire simultaneously on a specific creative, fable 5 flags it and recommends a specific replacement from the backup creative library, selected using the format rotation matrix (same buying trigger, different format type).

function 2: budget reallocation recommendations

fable 5 evaluates spend distribution across ad sets and creatives, identifies which ones are producing the best cost per qualified lead (not cost per lead, the distinction matters), and recommends budget shifts from underperformers to top performers. the recommendations follow the 20% scaling rule: increases of no more than 20% every 2-3 days on winning creatives.

function 3: creative brief generation for the next batch

this is the function that surprised us with how well it performs.

fable 5 cross-references the current performance data (which triggers are winning, which formats are performing, which awareness stages are converting) against the ICP briefing document and the competitive research from the last manus scan.

it produces a creative brief specifying:

- which buying triggers to prioritize in the next batch

- which format types to assign to each trigger

- which angle gaps exist (triggers or formats not yet tested)

- specific hook directions for each creative in the batch

- the awareness stage each creative should target

the brief is specific enough to hand directly to the creative production pipeline (AI or human) without additional interpretation.

if you want to see how we structure the fable 5 briefing document and the creative brief output for our client accounts, DM me "META" and i'll walk you through the setup.

function 4: competitive monitoring

fable 5 runs periodic checks against the meta ad library for competitor activity, flagging new creative formats entering the market, new angle categories being tested, and ads that have been running for 30+ days (a signal of sustained performance). the competitive data feeds into the creative brief generation.

function 5: weekly performance report

every monday morning, fable 5 produces a structured performance report covering: week-over-week trends by campaign and creative, the top 5 and bottom 5 creatives by qualified lead cost, fatigue status of every active creative, budget reallocation actions taken, and strategic recommendations for the coming week.

the report is formatted for the client to review, not for internal consumption. it replaces the 2-3 hours a human media buyer previously spent compiling the same report manually.

## what the human's role becomes

the human media buyers on our team have shifted from executors to auditors. their weekly workflow:

monday: review fable 5's weekly report. approve or override strategic recommendations. flag any edge cases where human judgment overrides the agent's logic (unusual market conditions, seasonal shifts, client-specific context the agent doesn't have).

wednesday: review the creative brief fable 5 generated for the next batch. validate the buying trigger priorities and format assignments. add human insight where the data doesn't tell the full story (a client mentioned a competitor pivot on a call, a seasonal trend the data hasn't picked up yet).

friday: quality check on the fatigue detection and budget reallocation actions taken during the week. verify the format rotation selections. review the competitive monitoring flags.

total human time per account per week: approximately 3 hours of review and oversight. down from 12-15 hours of manual execution before fable 5 was integrated.

## the compounding advantage

the structural advantage of running fable 5 as a media buyer compounds over time in three ways:

speed advantage. a human media buyer checks dashboards twice a day. fable 5 checks every 4 hours during peak windows. fatigue detected at hour 14 instead of hour 36 across a $500k/mo account translates to thousands saved in wasted spend during the decay period.

consistency advantage. a human media buyer has good days and bad days. their attention degrades after the sixth account review. their pattern recognition weakens when they're tired. fable 5 applies the same evaluation criteria with the same rigor to every creative in every account on every check. the 15th account gets the same quality of analysis as the first.

scale advantage. a human media buyer manages 5-8 accounts at the quality level we require. fable 5 manages 15+ accounts at the same quality level, with the human auditor reviewing the output rather than producing it. the unit economics of account management shift permanently when the execution is automated and the human provides strategic judgment.

## what fable 5 doesn't replace (and shouldn't)

fable 5 doesn't replace strategic thinking about the client's business. it doesn't replace the conversation where the client mentions a competitive threat, a product launch, or a market shift that changes the positioning. it doesn't replace the creative intuition that says "this buying trigger data is pointing toward a pain point we haven't named yet."

the agent runs the system. the human provides the context, the judgment, and the strategic direction that the system operates within.

the operators who treat fable 5 as a replacement for thinking will get mediocre results. the operators who treat fable 5 as a replacement for manual execution and give it proper context and oversight will have a structural advantage over every agency still doing this with human labor alone.

the advantage is not that fable 5 is smarter. the advantage is that fable 5 is faster, more consistent, and runs at a fraction of the cost, while the human strategic layer stays intact.
