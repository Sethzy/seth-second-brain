## Verbatim Text

every meta ad format explained in 1 article

meta ads run across dozens of distinct creative formats. each format reaches a different behavioral segment of your audience, performs differently by placement, and pairs best with specific angle types.

most operators run 1-2 formats and wonder why scaling hits a ceiling. the ceiling is format saturation. exhaust one format's audience and you've left the rest of the market untouched.

this is every format you need to know, what it does, and when to use it.

## awareness-based formats (by funnel stage)

problem-unaware ads

the goal: stop the scroll on someone who has no idea they need your service.

what it looks like: symptom-focused hook. no mention of your service or solution. the ad names a daily frustration the prospect feels, not a problem they've identified.

example hook: "why your close rate dropped 20% last quarter without you changing anything"

when to use: cold audiences. entering a new market. targeting a broad ICP who hasn't been educated on your category.

best formats: founder selfie video (feels organic, not like an ad), text-on-screen (documentary feel), static with surprising statistic.

problem-aware ads

the goal: confirm the problem exists, explain the root cause, position your mechanism as the solution.

what it looks like: acknowledges their frustration, explains why it's happening (root cause), introduces your approach as the fix.

example hook: "your CPL is $180 because you're optimizing for the wrong conversion event. here's what to switch to."

when to use: retargeting warm audiences. audiences who've engaged with your problem-unaware content. more sophisticated ICPs who already understand the space.

best formats: VSL or longer-form video (3-8 minutes), split-screen explainer, educational B-roll.

solution-aware ads

the goal: differentiate from other solutions the prospect is already aware of.

what it looks like: directly compares your approach to alternatives. uses contrast. makes competitors look inadequate.

example hook: "most meta ads agencies run 3 creatives per month. here's why we run 40 and what the difference generates."

when to use: competitive markets where prospects have seen multiple providers. retargeting people who've visited competitor pages.

best formats: founder-to-camera direct address, comparison static, case study B-roll.

product-aware ads

the goal: build trust with someone who knows you exist but hasn't committed.

what it looks like: heavy social proof, specific results, client testimonials, risk removal.

example hook: "[client name], $90k/mo to $784k MRR. here's the full breakdown."

when to use: mid-stage retargeting. audiences who've watched 50%+ of your VSL or visited your landing page.

best formats: client testimonial video (case study style), B-roll with voiceover, data visualization static.

most-aware ads

the goal: get someone who already knows everything to finally act.

what it looks like: direct urgency. calls out the delay. makes inaction feel costly.

example hook: "if you've been thinking about this for the last 3 weeks, here's what that cost you."

when to use: late-stage retargeting. people who've applied but not booked. people who booked but didn't show.

best formats: founder selfie (personal), direct text static (urgency), short-form video (30 seconds max).

## production-based formats (by creative style)

founder selfie video

what it is: founder filming themselves on iPhone, raw and direct to camera.

why it works: native to the feed. highest trust factor. doesn't pattern-match to "advertisement" in the viewer's brain.

best for: problem-unaware and most-aware stages. contrarian angles. personal brand building.

production notes: don't over-edit. rough cuts and imperfect lighting help. length: 30 seconds to 3 minutes depending on awareness level.

talking head (polished)

what it is: founder or spokesperson on camera with controlled lighting, good audio, and minimal editing.

why it works: professional credibility without the coldness of branded video. works well for authority and social proof angles.

best for: solution-aware and product-aware stages. B2B audiences. high-ticket services.

B-roll with voiceover

what it is: footage of the service in action, the founder working, the team, the deliverables, paired with narration.

why it works: shows social proof through action rather than telling. visually interesting. works across placements.

best for: demonstration angles. "day in the life" social proof. process transparency ads.

split-screen explainer

what it is: founder on the lower third of the screen, visual aid (whiteboard, iPad drawing, screen recording, Miro board) on the upper two-thirds.

why it works: educational format. gives the viewer something to look at beyond the face. breaks the "talking head" pattern.

best for: mechanism explanation. problem-aware educational angles. "here's how this works" frameworks.

green screen with context

what it is: founder filmed against a green screen, background replaced with a relevant visual: competitor's ad, industry news headline, screenshot of data, client results dashboard.

why it works: adds visual evidence without on-location filming. creates contrast between the founder and the thing they're talking about.

best for: contrarian angles. "here's what your competitor is doing wrong" angles. data-backed authority content.

UGC style (user generated content)

what it is: real client filmed on their own phone sharing their experience. minimal branding. casual, authentic feel.

why it works: highest believability of any creative format. looks like something a friend would share, not an advertisement.

best for: testimonial angles. social proof campaigns. retargeting audiences who are close to conversion.

production note: don't over-script. give clients 3-4 bullet points to cover. let them speak naturally. the imperfections increase trust.

static data visualization

what it is: clean graphic with a specific stat, chart, comparison table, or before/after metric.

why it works: authority through specificity. datadriven ICPs respond to concrete proof over emotional appeals.

best for: loss aversion angles. proof-based retargeting. B2B audiences with financial decision-making roles.

example: bar chart showing $84k vs $51k annual insurance cost. bold text: "the overpayment your broker doesn't want you to see."

story-native ads

what it is: vertical format (9:16), text heavy, designed to look like someone's instagram or facebook story.

why it works: completely native to story placement. browser's ad filter doesn't activate because it pattern-matches to organic content.

best for: story placement exclusively. quick wins and urgency messaging. most-aware audiences.

carousel

what it is: multiple slides telling a sequential story, each with its own image and caption.

why it works: higher engagement through swipe interaction. lets you tell a longer story without losing attention. good for mechanism education.

best for: problem-aware to solution-aware transitions. step-by-step frameworks. before/after journey narratives.

educational (listicle style)

what it is: text on screen in bullet or numbered format, often narrated, sharing tactical insights.

why it works: massive value delivery in a short format. positions the brand as the expert without an explicit pitch. captures content-seeking audiences.

best for: problem-aware cold traffic. organic-first audiences. authority building in the feed.

straight pitch (direct response)

what it is: no storytelling. no education. pure direct offer. "here's what we do, here's the result, here's how to get it."

why it works: efficient for most-aware audiences. no wasted time. respects that the prospect already knows what they need.

best for: hot retargeting. audiences who've been through the full nurture sequence. late-stage conversion campaigns.

AI-generated creative

what it is: fully AI-produced visuals (chatgpt images 2.0, midjourney, seedance 2.0 animations) as the creative asset.

why it works: faster production. infinite variation. visually distinct from human-produced content.

best for: testing angles quickly before investing in human production. abstract concept visualization. scale testing.

current limitations: still struggles with ultra-realistic human faces in complex scenarios. works best for data visualizations, product mockups, B2B service imagery, and animated concept explanations.

## the format testing matrix

every angle should be tested across at minimum 3 formats before declaring it a winner or loser.

the fractional CFO client's "why profitable agencies go broke" angle was tested as:

- founder selfie: 3.2% CTR

- data viz static: 6.1% CTR

- split-screen with calculator: 5.4% CTR

the same angle. different results. the data viz static outperformed the founder selfie by 90%. if they'd tested only one format, they'd have had a decision based on the worst result.

test the format, not just the angle.
