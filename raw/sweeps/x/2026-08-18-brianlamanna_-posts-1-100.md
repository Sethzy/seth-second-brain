It’s sort of BS. But not in a bad way. And yes... this may enrage some.

Oversimplified Situation: It’s 2020. Pretend your company has 100 opportunities and 1,000 accounts that are strong fits.

Each seller can handle 10 opps and 100 accounts each at a time.

In 2020, you’d need 10 sellers to cover this territory effectively.
 
But from 2020 to 2030, AI comes along and now… 

 2x increase in deal capacity
 2x increase in account capacity 

(Due to reduction in admin tasks. Think updating CRM, writing follow up emails, sequencing prospects, power dialers, etc.)

Now each seller can handle 20 opps and 200 accounts each.
 
In 2030, you’d need only 5 sellers to cover this territory effectively.

If you are CEO, why carry 10 sellers that cost you $250K annually each?

Instead, you can carry your 5 best sellers with the best win rates.

You can even give them all a raise in the process. 

 𝗧𝗟𝗗𝗥:
 -AI won’t replace the seller directly in the sales process
 -AI will make each seller much more productive and efficient

Leading to organizations having to carry less SDRs and AE over time. 

This will be slow and gradual. No mass firings. Instead, retaining the top talent and empowering them with tech.

Agree? Disagree? 

 P.S. This is similar to other tech advancements like machines on the assembly line. Does this replace all factory workers? No. It allows a company to have more specialized and strategic workers. And less overall.

Here are some of my favorite questions I draw from in a first call:

1.. “How did you determine ___ was your #1 priority?”

2. “How is ____ showing up in the business today?”

3. “Where is that today and where are you hoping to get it to?” [Current state / Ideal state]

4. “Have you done the math on what that would be worth if you got to ideal state?”

5. “What have you tried so far to solve that?”

6. “I don’t want to ask a loaded question but this seems like a really, really big priority. Silly question but fast forward 90 days... are we okay if we haven’t addressed this challenge yet? Or are we committed to solving this asap?”

7. “What happens if we don’t get there?” (keep brevity and ask confidently)

8. Empathy question to insert: “What about this issue is something that others you’ve explained it to, just don’t seem to understand?” 

Great discovery will never follow this path in a linear order.

Script out a few that you can lean back on as needed.

Then focus most on active listening during.

The #1 skill I've been working on this year:

Asking "why" 5 times in a row, without prospects hating me.

Most discovery I see stops at level 2. Prospect gives you a pain, you go 1 step deeper, then get excited to show them how you solve it. 

Level 5 is where the money is.

Example from earlier this month:
Level 1: "We need to improve our coaching"
Level 2: "Our win rates are down 5%"
Level 3: "New competitor is taking market share"
Level 4: "We're going to miss our end-of-year target"
Level 5: "We're not going to secure our Series C funding round"

Same deal. Two completely different conversations.

At level 2, we're a "help us coach a bit better" tool. 

All the cheap AI notetakers look pretty comparable and we get into feature-function.

At level 5, we're the Series C solution. $50K vs $100K is nothing.

The tactic isn't asking "why" over and over like a toddler.

It's saying "this might be a silly question, but why is getting win rates back to 5% really important when you think about the greater business?"

Prospects only get annoyed by AEs who never earn the right to ask it.
