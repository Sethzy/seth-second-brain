You have to treat it like the 1st deal, you will ever close.

It will never just be given to you.

1. Obvious Business Case

First and foremost - you need to exceed your current numbers. Nothing else matters here if you aren't a top, consistent performer. Get it done.

2. Build Champions

You should have very close relationships with your manager and other teammates that would vouch for you. The type that would fight back if they were told "you aren't ready."

3. Uncover the decision process

Understand clearly what has to take place for you to get promoted. If there are certain targets you have to hit or time thresholds in place.

4. Identify Decision Makers + Get to Power

Learn who the real decision is ultimately up to. Ask to meet with them and others apart of the decision. Make your intentions known that you want to get promoted. Learn what reservations they have about you and what gaps lie.

5. Seek out the risk and build an action plan

After you hear what reservations they have or gaps standing in the way of your promotion, build an action plan. Document everything you do. Heck, even build a mutual success plan with them. Wouldn't that be impressive?

Instead of just continuing to tell them you want the role of AE.

Make sure you SHOW them you are ready for that role.

Take the process by the horns.

#### Verbatim Text

The trap most reps fall into with pain discovery.

They ask "what" 10 different ways and never ask "why" once.

"What tools are you using?"
"What's your win rate?"
"What's your process?"
"What are you trying to improve?"

None of that gets you to real pain. 

The unlock typically leads with this instead: why.

"Why is that important?"
"Why now?"
"Why does this priority trump the others?"
"Why is your CEO focused on this specifically?"

Whats give you a baseline understanding of what they're going after. 

Whys give you a business case and urgency. 

Happy Friday + EOQ all! 

P.S. Lots of exceptions here like "What is going on in the business that is making this your top focus?" ... but if you get to the root of those, you are still uncovering WHY.

Get asked how I write cold emails all the time.

Not because they're perfect. Because I ship quality ones in 5-7 minutes.

5 components. Every time:

1. Observation - recent business trigger (news, launch, initiative)
2. Problem - specific persona pain, tied back to the observation
3. Social proof - short case study, video, or customer quote (optional)
4. CTA - conversational, not "Tuesday at 2pm?"
5. PS - something so specific to them AI could never write it

The PS is a big part of it. 

When execs get 126 emails a day and scan yours in 5 seconds, they scan the top and the bottom.

If your PS says "Fun fact, I used to work at your main competitor BrightEdge, small world" - now they're reading the middle too.

Spend 20 mins on 1 perfect email? That's 2 emails an hour.

Spend 7 mins on a good email? That's 8-10 emails an hour.

The math wins every single time.
