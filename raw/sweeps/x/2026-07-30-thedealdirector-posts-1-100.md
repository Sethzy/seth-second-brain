This difficult renewal handled by the Cursor GTM team is a good example of why you reframe the conversation around value instead of price.

The first mistake is failing to position the obvious gap between consumption pricing, which is now standard across these contracts, and a legacy fixed price that is no longer sustainable.

The second mistake is failing to spot the account's rising Anthropic usage ($10M ARR!) and to challenge the assumption that Claude Code is the best harness for it. It isn't.

The third mistake is caving on price and signing a loss-making "save the account" deal for a customer who is already moving all their usage to Anthropic.

Driving a value sale does far more than justify the price. It creates a different type of relationship, advisory rather than salesy. It lets you establish what "fair" looks like. It also gives you a strong reason to walk away from a bad deal: they are simply trying to squeeze free tokens out of us.

And we have not even touched on the harder counterarguments here: vendor lock-in, efficiency gains, cloud agents, and custom coding models, among others. 

All of them reshape the adoption curve and what engineering teams actually get out of the subscription.
