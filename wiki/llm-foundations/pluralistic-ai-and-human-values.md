# Pluralistic AI And Human Values

> Sources: Mira Murati, 2026-07-10
> Raw: [Mira Murati on pluralistic AI values](../../raw/intentional/x/2075621073308311701-miramurati-today-we-share-the-worldview-behind-our-mission-human-values-don-t-average-out.md)

## Overview

Mira Murati's stated worldview rejects the idea that human values can be reduced to a single average or that local knowledge can be fully centralized. The proposed desirable future is pluralistic: many AIs, shaped in different places by the people they serve, can disagree in ways that preserve human diversity rather than flattening it.

## Core Claims

- Human values are plural rather than one universal, average preference.
- Local knowledge matters and should not be assumed to fit a centralized model of what is good.
- AI systems can be shaped by the communities and people they serve.
- Disagreement among AI systems may be a feature of a healthy pluralistic future, not only a defect to eliminate.

This is a worldview statement, not a complete governance framework. It leaves open implementation questions about safety boundaries, interoperability, accountability, rights, and how to resolve conflicts when locally shaped systems affect people outside their immediate community.

## Questions For Evaluation

- Which values or policy choices should be locally configurable, and which require shared global safeguards?
- Who legitimately represents the people whose values shape a system?
- How can a pluralistic ecosystem remain auditable and accountable when systems disagree?
- What technical and institutional mechanisms prevent local adaptation from becoming exclusion, harm, or arbitrary control?
