# AI GTM Ops Cleanup Starter Wedge

> Sources: [Workflow Hustle While Job Hunting](workflow-hustle-while-job-hunting.md); [AI Services Lead Generation](ai-services-lead-generation.md); [AI-Native Services Companies](ai-native-services-companies.md)
> Archived: 2026-06-25

## Overview

This is a point-in-time side-hustle answer to the practical question: what is a simpler version of an AI-native services company Seth can start now? The answer is not FDA regulatory services or an AI-native law firm. The simpler wedge is an AI workflow audit plus one done-for-you fix, ideally in GTM operations where Seth can personally deliver the outcome this week and turn the repeated pain into prompts, scripts, checklists, and proof-of-work.

## Examples From The YC Transcript

The YC AI-native services transcript named or implied these business categories:

- Insurance carriers rebuilt from scratch with AI.
- Law firms rebuilt from scratch with AI.
- Tax, audit, insurance, mortgages, parts of healthcare, and parts of logistics.
- FDA regulatory services for biotech and medtech.
- **Panacea:** a YC company providing FDA regulatory services by pairing experienced FDA consultants with an AI platform.
- **General Legal Team:** an AI-native law firm YC backed, combining law firm experience with technical leadership from CaseText.
- Insurance licensing as one possible reason to acquire an existing business, though the default warning was to build rather than buy.

Those examples are useful as a direction of travel, but most are too complex or regulated for a first side-hustle test.

## Simpler Starter Offer

Start with:

> "I will inspect one repetitive workflow, show where AI can save time, and implement one small fix in a week."

This keeps the promise small enough to deliver manually. It also creates the right learning loop: feel the annoying workflow by hand, identify repeated steps, then automate only what repeats.

Good first workflow categories:

- Inbound leads to qualification, summary, and drafted replies.
- Customer emails to category labels, response drafts, and CRM/tasks.
- Meeting notes to follow-up email, CRM update, and next steps.
- Invoices or receipts to extracted spreadsheet/accounting prep.
- Intake notes to quote or proposal drafts.
- YouTube, podcast, webinar, or article content to posts and newsletters.

## Best First Lane For Seth

The most Seth-shaped starter lane is:

**AI GTM Ops Cleanup for small B2B teams.**

The buyer is a founder, consultant, agency, or small B2B team with messy leads, call notes, websites, LinkedIn lists, and follow-up gaps. The deliverable is not "AI transformation." The deliverable is cleaned and prioritized GTM work.

Why this fits:

- Low regulation.
- The buyer already values leads, follow-up, and pipeline hygiene.
- Seth can deliver the work manually before automating it.
- Codex, browser work, spreadsheets, CRM cleanup, and the Second Brain all help.
- Every project becomes job-search proof-of-work: before/after data, workflow map, outreach examples, and operating lessons.

## Tiny Pilot Package

The first package can be:

> "Send me 20 leads or your messy CRM export. I will return a cleaned, prioritized list with account notes, suggested next action, and 10 personalized outreach drafts."

Concrete outputs:

- Cleaned prospect/account list.
- Short account notes.
- Suggested next action per account.
- Ten personalized outreach drafts.
- Optional follow-up reminders or CRM import format.
- A short before/after note showing what was cleaned, enriched, or prioritized.

Early price:

- First proof: roughly `$200-$500`, especially if Seth needs testimonials and examples.
- Repeatable sprint after proof: roughly `$750-$1.5k`.

The price should be framed around the useful outcome, not model use or hours. The first few projects can be underpriced for learning, but they should not become open-ended free labor.

## Operating Rules

- Pick one buyer group for the first test, such as solo consultants, small agencies, boutique B2B startups, recruiting firms, or local service businesses with inbound leads.
- Keep scope to one workflow and one week.
- Do the first run manually enough to understand edge cases.
- Track time spent, human minutes per output, quality variance, and what could become a reusable prompt/script.
- Package every project as proof-of-work: workflow map, input sample, output sample, what broke, and next automation step.
- Avoid heavily regulated or judgment-dense work until Seth has stronger domain fluency or a partner with credibility.
