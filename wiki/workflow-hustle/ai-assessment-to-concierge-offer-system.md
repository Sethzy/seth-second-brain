## Overview

This is the complete assessment-to-retainer operating model captured from the supplied AI Operator Academy materials. The commercial ladder is a free mini assessment, a paid full AI Tools Assessment, implementation or process-redesign work, and an ongoing AI Concierge retainer. Its central delivery rule is AOA: audit the manual process, optimize it by removing unnecessary work, and only then automate the cleaned-up workflow.

The playbooks contain promotional performance and margin claims. Preserve those claims as source-reported examples, not independently verified forecasts. The durable knowledge is the offer architecture, discovery method, diagnostic logic, artifact design, post-call workflow, and renewal ledger.

## The Offer Ladder

Each rung earns the right to offer the next:

1. **Free mini assessment:** two short meetings focused on one bottleneck and one recommendation.
2. **Paid AI Tools Assessment:** a deeper discovery and a nine-slide report covering three to seven opportunities, prioritization, tools, implementation steps, and financial impact. The cited anchor price is $999.
3. **Implementation or redesign:** done-with-you or done-for-you work on the highest-priority opportunity. The materials cite examples around $3,000-$3,500 for knowledge systems or process redesign and a broader $3,000-$10,000 implementation range.
4. **AI Concierge:** recurring advisory and build support, usually two 45-minute calls per month plus asynchronous voice access. The materials cite $1,000-$2,000 per month, with higher cadence priced higher.

The free work is deliberately bounded. It provides one diagnosis and one tool; the paid assessment expands the analysis; implementation makes the chosen workflow work; the retainer repeats the operating loop across new bottlenecks.

## Value Test And Diagnostic Logic

Every opportunity must improve at least one business lever:

- **Effectiveness:** make or protect revenue.
- **Efficiency:** reclaim time or reduce operating effort.
- **Quality:** improve the customer or product experience.

For an initial assessment, prioritize the task with the highest combination of frequency and friction, weighted toward the lever the owner says matters most. Route the resulting problem into one of three solution categories:

- A common, high-frequency task with a mature product category may fit an off-the-shelf tool.
- A judgment, research, or writing task without a clean vertical tool may fit a general AI work surface such as Claude Cowork.
- A repeatable proprietary workflow may justify a custom skill or implementation.

The category is a hypothesis, not a forced answer. Tool fit, data access, security, reliability, integration cost, change management, and measurable ROI still require validation.

## Free Mini Assessment

### Meeting One: Fact-Finding

Keep the first 15-minute meeting diagnostic. Do not improvise a tool recommendation before understanding the bottleneck.

1. Ask which outcome matters most in the next 30 days: more money, more time, or happier customers.
2. Ask which task the owner or team repeats most often.
3. Ask where work slows down, gets stuck, or falls through the cracks.
4. Quantify the hours per week and identify who performs the work.
5. Ask the magic-wand question: if this one thing ran itself, what would change?
6. Restate the bottleneck in one sentence and book the follow-up.

### Between Meetings

Choose one bottleneck. Research one credible solution. Bring back the tool or approach, its cost, and the first action the prospect can take that week. The original playbook points to Futurepedia and There's An AI For That for category discovery, but recommendations should be independently validated before delivery.

### Meeting Two: Prescription And Choice

Deliver the promised recommendation without holding it hostage. Then let the prospect choose the support level: run with it independently, set it up together, or hand off implementation. This transitions naturally into the full paid assessment when the prospect wants a broader map rather than one fix.

## Paid AI Tools Assessment

The paid assessment is diagnosis sold as a standalone product, not disguised proposal work.

### Discovery

Run a question-led conversation and capture a transcript. Useful prompts include walking through the previous workday, identifying dreaded tasks, locating queues and dropped handoffs, understanding existing tools, and quantifying time or revenue impact. Avoid pitching during discovery.

### Analysis

Extract pain points, map workflows, estimate frequency and friction, and score opportunities on impact versus effort. Use AI to accelerate transcript analysis and pattern recognition, but verify recommendations, costs, integrations, and calculations before client delivery.

### Nine-Slide Deliverable

The saved HTML artifact defines the report shape:

1. Cover and assessment context.
2. Executive summary: core pain, desired outcome, time reclaimed, and primary focus.
3. Impact-versus-effort matrix.
4. Quick wins.
5. Recommended solutions with use case, cost, setup effort, and time saved.
6. Four-day quick-win plan.
7. Larger follow-on opportunities.
8. Financial impact and tool costs.
9. Next steps and review call.

### Review Call

Ask which recommendation is most urgent, whether the client wants to do it themselves or get help, and their desired timeline. The goal is to select the next action, not force an upsell.

## Audit, Optimize, Automate

AOA is the reusable delivery loop for every implementation:

1. **Audit:** observe the current process as it actually runs, including exceptions, handoffs, inputs, tools, and failure points.
2. **Optimize:** remove redundant or low-value steps, clarify ownership, and simplify the workflow before adding software.
3. **Automate:** automate only the stable, repeatable pieces; preserve human judgment, approval, and escalation where risk warrants it.

The supplied example of reducing an 18-step advertising workflow to nine steps illustrates that process redesign can be valuable even without automation. Treat the associated pricing and outcome as a source claim rather than a universal benchmark.

## AI Concierge Delivery System

### Engagement Setup

- Invoice and collect the first month before beginning.
- Require an intake form before the first call.
- Give each client a dedicated workspace or hub.
- Use a call recorder/notetaker with client consent.
- Establish the asynchronous communication channel and response-time expectation.

### Intake Form

Collect business basics, time sinks, owner bottlenecks, dropped work, recurring processes across Sales, Operations, Finance, Marketing, and Intelligence, the top ten software tools, current AI usage, team adoption, the value of ten reclaimed hours per week, the desired 90-day outcome, and call logistics.

The 90-day outcome is the engagement's north star. Each build should connect back to that result.

### Client Hub

The hub is both the operating surface and the renewal ledger. It should include:

- Quick links and engagement details.
- Open action items for both sides.
- A reverse-chronological call log.
- Recording and summary links.
- Three key takeaways per call.
- Tools, skills, workflows, and context files built during each session.
- A plain-language statement of what changed and why it matters.

The visible build log makes progress legible. When possible, add outcome measures such as hours saved, cycle-time reduction, volume handled, errors avoided, revenue protected, or customer-experience improvement.

### First Call

For a client new to the chosen AI work surface, use the first session to establish context, instructions, tool connections, security boundaries, and a small number of relevant recurring tasks. For an already capable client, begin the AOA loop on the highest-priority bottleneck.

### Post-Call Automation

The source playbook proposes one orchestrator that runs two tasks in parallel:

- Draft the follow-up email from the transcript, including takeaways, work completed, and next actions.
- Update the client hub with the same structured evidence.

Both outputs require a human checkpoint before sending or publishing. The target is a useful recap within minutes of the call, not unreviewed automation.

### Between Calls And Renewal

Use the asynchronous channel to resolve small blockers between scheduled calls. The source suggests a 12-business-hour service level while responding faster when practical. Renewal should be earned through a growing ledger of completed work and measurable outcomes rather than generic access.

## Objection Reframes

Use these as conversational frames, then pause and let the prospect respond:

| Objection | Reframe |
|---|---|
| "I can use ChatGPT myself." | The service is workflow diagnosis, prioritization, implementation, and adoption—not model access. |
| "AI is overhyped." | Start with one concrete, testable win and judge it on observed results. |
| "It is too expensive." | Compare cost with time returned, revenue protected or created, and the cost of continued delay. |
| "I do not have time." | The implementation should remove work from the client's plate; scope the client's required involvement explicitly. |
| "I tried AI and got garbage." | Diagnose and prioritize before building, then validate the workflow against real inputs and acceptance criteria. |
| "My business is too specialized." | Map the actual workflow and domain constraints rather than applying a generic template. |
| "Will this replace or scare my team?" | Frame the work around removing tasks and bottlenecks, while involving affected employees in redesign and adoption. |
| "I need to think or ask my partner." | Clarify the decision, stakeholders, cost of delay, evidence needed, and a specific follow-up date without manufacturing pressure. |

These reframes work best when the discovery call already established the prospect's own numbers and priorities.

## Economics And Evidence Guardrails

The sources report conversion rates of 30-50% from free mini assessments to paid work, 50-60% implementation interest after review calls, $8,000 in MRR within ten days, blended rates above $1,000 per hour, and margins near 100%. These figures are promotional and have not been independently verified.

Use them only as hypotheses when designing experiments. Track Seth's actual funnel:

- Outreach attempts and response rate.
- Mini assessments booked and completed.
- Paid assessments sold.
- Assessments converted to implementation.
- Retainers started, expanded, renewed, and churned.
- Delivery hours, software costs, gross margin, and unpaid support time.
- Promised versus realized client outcomes.

Calculate ROI from transparent client-specific assumptions. Separate gross time-value estimates from cash savings or new revenue, and include implementation time, recurring tool cost, adoption risk, and confidence ranges.
