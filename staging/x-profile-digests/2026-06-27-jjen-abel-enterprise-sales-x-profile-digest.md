## Promote To Wiki

### High-Signal Enterprise Sales: Co-Authored Deal Craft

Jen Abel's strongest recurring pattern is that enterprise selling is not polished vendor performance. It is off-script deal craft with buyer-side co-authorship. The seller's job is to help the buyer shape the internal storyline, funding path, pricing/invoicing explanation, implementation/risk narrative, and political case that lets the deal move through the buyer's company.

Promote into `high-signal-enterprise-sales.md`:

- Group or team demos too early are a major deal-killer unless the seller has already done one-on-one context collection with most attendees.
- A deal is not truly qualified when the CRM says it is; useful qualifiers include internal pressure, access to a cell/text channel, N or N-1 influence, and buyer-side insider context.
- "Send collateral," "send a case study," "let's take a step back," a next step two-plus weeks out, or a buyer refusing direct/private channels can be stall signals.
- Enterprise sales moves from persuasion to project management after the first call. The seller must hand-hold the buying process, procurement, stakeholder narrative, and internal memo.
- The buyer and seller should feel like the same team trying to navigate the buyer's company, not two sides trading vendor terms.

### Founder-Led Enterprise Sales: Founder Energy Without Founder Bottleneck

Jen's corpus aligns tightly with the Amanda Zhu founder-led sales page, but adds a sharper first-sales-hire lens. In early enterprise sales, the founder is often the product because early adopters buy access to vision, roadmap, judgment, and confidence. The first sales hire should therefore be able to "method-act" the founder: carry the vision, pattern-match the market, and make buyers feel the company's intensity even when the founder is not present.

Promote into `founder-led-enterprise-sales-playbooks.md`:

- Founder-led sales is a competitive advantage until the motion is extracted into judgment, talk tracks, qualification rules, and buyer-side narrative craft.
- Non-traditional sales talent, consultants, forward-deployed engineers, or other high-agency operators may outperform logo-trained sales reps when the motion requires creativity and founder-like context.
- A strong first sales hire is not mainly "sales charisma"; the useful skill is reading between the lines, collecting nuanced intel, co-authoring the buyer narrative, and improvising the storyline.
- Well-known-logo sales backgrounds can be misleading because brand and marketing may have done much of the selling work.

### AI-Native Account Intelligence: Qualification Signals And Red Flags

Jen's posts make the account-intelligence schema more concrete. The system should not only store generic signals; it should track evidence that the buyer can move the deal internally.

Promote into `ai-native-account-intelligence.md`:

- Qualification fields: cell/text access, N or N-1 involvement, internal pressure/incentive, buyer's own questions, willingness to share internal context, and who owns the internal narrative.
- Red-flag fields: asks for collateral or case studies as the next step, group demo too early, no direct channel, next step more than two weeks out, communicating through too many layers, no camera/private rapport, or only a user with no buyer access.
- Deal design fields: whether the buyer helped define the demo/pilot success criteria, whether the seller understands the buying process before setting GTM steps, and whether pricing has been tied to funding/defense inside the buyer.

### Agentic GTM Workflows: Automation Must Preserve Deal Craft

Jen's profile is a useful counterweight to over-automated outbound. Her durable advice is that enterprise buyers read bloated "corpo" messages as sales-y, ignore generic attachments, and respond better to direct text-like communication, context, and co-authored assets.

Promote into `agentic-gtm-campaign-workflows.md`:

- Agent-generated outreach should get shorter and more direct as account context improves.
- Collateral, one-pagers, Looms, and generic slides are weak unless they are co-authored or highly contextualized to the buyer's internal story.
- Enterprise campaign agents should support one-on-one stakeholder context gathering before recommending a group demo.
- The more technical the executive pitch, the faster the seller risks being delegated downward; campaign assets should frame executive advantage and influence, not only user value.
