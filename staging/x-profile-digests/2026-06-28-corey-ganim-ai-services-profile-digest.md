### AI Services Lead Generation: Sell The Leak, Not AI

The strongest repeated pattern is that beginners should not sell generic AI automation. They should find a visible workflow leak and sell a narrow audit or fix around that leak.

Promote into `ai-services-lead-generation.md`:

- Visible leak discovery sources: Google reviews, weak website forms, downloadable/manual PDFs, delayed quote processes, missed calls, dormant CRM records, abandoned quotes, slow lead response, and repeated customer questions.
- Outreach should lead with the prospect's known pain, not with "AI." The audit uncovers details after the prospect already recognizes the leak.
- The first offer ladder is mini-assessment -> paid AI assessment/audit -> implementation sprint -> managed retainer or productized agent.
- The audit is a product, not unpaid pre-work. It should show where the business is losing time, losing revenue, which workflow to fix first, which tools fit, what should stay human, what can be automated now, and what needs cleanup first.
- Corey repeatedly frames $999 as a paid assessment price point, with higher-ticket implementation or managed-service upsells. Treat those as positioning examples, not universal pricing benchmarks.

### Workflow Hustle While Job Hunting: Practical Starter Sequence

The profile sweep aligns with the existing workflow-hustle page. The practical sequence is:

1. Pick one buyer group.
2. Find one expensive workflow leak.
3. Sell a small audit.
4. Fix the highest-value workflow.
5. Turn the fix into a reusable skill, runbook, or agent.
6. Report proof monthly if the engagement becomes recurring.

Corey repeatedly warns against automating chaos. The correct order is audit the workflow, remove waste, write the clean version down, automate repeatable pieces, and keep judgment with a human.
