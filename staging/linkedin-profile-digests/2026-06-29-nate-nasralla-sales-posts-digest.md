## Main Learnings To Promote

### Decision Confidence Is The Seller's Job

Nate's strongest recurring point is that an AE's job is not to keep a conversation alive; it is to help the buyer and champion reach a confident yes or a clean no. The current buyer tension in the corpus is speed pressure versus fear that fast-changing technology will make today's decision obsolete. The antidote is a clear point of view, an internal business case, and forwardable messaging that helps the champion guide the buyer-side process.

Promote into `high-signal-enterprise-sales.md`:

- Ask whether the buyer understands why the business has to act now, what changes if they wait, and what outcome the seller uniquely enables.
- Treat "let's keep talking" as a stalled-decision state, not healthy momentum.
- Build the next step around buyer decision confidence, not seller activity.

### The Business Case Is The Sales Process

The corpus repeatedly argues that business-case creation is not a late-stage ROI activity. It is the operating medium for discovery, demo design, multithreading, forecasting, internal champion enablement, procurement/legal/security context, and post-sale handoff. One-page docs are valuable because they make discovery gaps visible, preserve customer language, and travel inside internal meetings where the seller is absent.

Promote into `high-signal-enterprise-sales.md` and `ai-native-account-intelligence.md`:

- Track whether each serious opportunity has a written business case, who edited it, what evidence it contains, and which internal priority it supports.
- Treat customer redlines, comments, and edits as buying-process evidence.
- Evaluate deal quality by the clarity of the buyer's internal story, not by the number of meetings or sales-room views.

### Docs Carry Influence Better Than Decks

Nate's docs-versus-decks pattern is practical: docs are for writing the narrative before a meeting, sharpening the live talk track, and sending the updated version afterward; slides are visual aids during the conversation and should stay sparse. The durable lesson is that the async buyer process needs complete thoughts, not vendor-branded asset dumps.

Promote into `agentic-gtm-campaign-workflows.md`:

- Agent-generated assets should default to short customer-language docs, not generic slide decks.
- Before a seller generates collateral, the workflow should ask what the buyer needs to repeat internally in under 60 seconds.
- A "digital sales room" is weak unless it contains a clear account-specific message and co-authored internal story.

### Account-Based Selling Is A Living-Docs Chain

The account-based model in this export is a sequence of account-specific docs: POV, account plan, demo brief, business case, leader deal brief, mutual success plan, and CS handoff. Each artifact carries context forward into the next stage and gives the internal GTM team a shared surface.

Promote into `agentic-gtm-campaign-workflows.md`:

- A campaign agent should output stage-specific artifacts, not just outreach copy.
- The account plan should feed the demo brief; the demo brief should feed the business case; the business case should feed deal review, mutual success, and CS handoff.
- "Content is evidence" because the content shows what the buyer has actually validated or corrected.

### Executive POV Has To Attach To Priority

The corpus is strong on exec meetings: a seller must arrive with a non-obvious insight about a metric or priority the executive already cares about. The seller should frame the decision as a tradeoff against other current priorities and get a specific owner/referral if the conversation needs to move downward.

Promote into `high-signal-enterprise-sales.md`:

- Build POV from a driver graph: executive-owned metric, active board/priority path, current change, unique response, good outcome, avoided bad outcome.
- Ask whether the deal is the meeting, in the meeting, or merely after the meeting.
- In exec meetings, confirm the decision path, named owners, and next internal step rather than settling for generic interest.

### Mid-Funnel Signals Beat Static Stage Names

Nate's repeated mid-funnel signal is time between meetings. If a buyer will not schedule the next step while the topic is live, the deal may need nurture, reframing, or a different internal priority. Forecast and QBR hygiene should therefore focus on evidence of buying behavior, not rep optimism.

Promote into `ai-native-account-intelligence.md`:

- Track time-between-meetings, whether the next step was scheduled live, and whether the buyer chose "tomorrow" versus "in a few weeks."
- Require deal reviews to start with the message headline, evidence of specific buying behaviors, and one-page links to supporting material.
- Map stage exit criteria to buyer actions, not seller actions.

### AI For Sales Needs Persistent Deal Context

The AI-specific posts argue that disposable workflow agents are limited because they do not retain deal context or compare patterns across closed-won and closed-lost cycles. The more interesting architecture is a persistent GTM context layer that can answer what has worked in similar situations, recommend a next move, and draft the appropriate artifact.

Promote into `agentic-gtm-campaign-workflows.md` and `ai-native-account-intelligence.md`:

- Measure agent impact by revenue outcomes and deal progression, not only tool runs or token usage.
- Preserve deal context across emails, calls, Slack, docs, and stage outcomes so the system can learn from patterns.
- Keep human approval for commercial actions, but let the system surface next-best artifacts from similar successful deals.
