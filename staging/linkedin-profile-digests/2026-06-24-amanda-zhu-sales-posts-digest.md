## Durable Themes

- Founder-led sales is the learning phase. Amanda repeatedly frames early selling as the only way to learn buyer language, qualification, demos, objections, and what should eventually become the playbook.
- The enterprise motion is staged and multi-threaded. Her strongest posts describe discovery, use-case demos, PoCs, Slack/internal channels, technical validation, security/procurement/legal review, stakeholder mapping, executive onboarding, and champion rescue.
- Demos should prove the buyer's use case, not tour the product. The demo pattern is to learn the problem, show only the relevant workflow, and let the buyer define proof.
- Qualification is often disqualification. She argues that founders should repel poor-fit buyers, get to no quickly, and avoid months of maybe-pipeline.
- Trials and discounts are sales design choices. Her posts push against open-ended free trials, loose terms, and being too easy to buy from when those patterns attract weak buyers or hide missing urgency.
- Messaging is a revenue lever. Several posts tie the jump from $1M to $10M ARR to positioning the company as an API/infrastructure layer rather than a consumer notetaker.
- Sales-org scale means extracting founder judgment. The handoff to AEs depends on talk tracks, objection handling, demo structure, deal reviews, onboarding, and knowing when a founder should stop owning the full motion.
- Account intelligence matters most when deals get large. Posts about champions going dark, wrong stakeholders, deal percentages, flying to prospects, and C-level language all reinforce the need for source-backed deal state.
